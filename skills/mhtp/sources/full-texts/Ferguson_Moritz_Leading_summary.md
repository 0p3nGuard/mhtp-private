# Leading: Learning from Life and My Years at Manchester United — Sir Alex Ferguson with Michael Moritz, 2015
*Own-prose reference summary for MHTP (copyright-safe distillation, not verbatim). Part of Matt's canon — Matt specifically flagged **Moritz's epilogue** on the Sequoia parallels. Source book not in repo; summary grounded in the public record.*

## What it is
A leadership book built from Sir Alex Ferguson's 26 years managing Manchester United (1986–2013), co-written with **Sir Michael Moritz** (chairman of Sequoia Capital — see also his talk at `Moritz_Michael_Sequoia_StanfordGSB.txt`). Ferguson supplies the football-management experience; Moritz shapes it into transferable leadership principles and writes the epilogue tying it to business.

## Ferguson's leadership themes (the body of the book)
Organized around recurring principles, roughly:
- **Listening & watching.** As he aged, Ferguson talked less and observed more — reading body language, spotting who was off, delegating the physical coaching so he could watch the whole board. Attention as the core managerial act.
- **Standards & discipline.** Non-negotiable standards, punctuality, control of the environment; never ceding authority — the willingness to move on even a superstar to protect the standard of the whole.
- **Developing young talent.** The long-game bet on youth (the "Class of '92," Beckham spotted young) and building an academy pipeline — patient, organic talent development as durable advantage.
- **The team over the individual.** Continuous, imperceptible squad renewal — replenishing the bench so outsiders only ever see the winning team, never the rebuild.
- **Motivation & communication.** Tailoring feedback to the person; the calibrated use of praise, criticism, and the occasional detonation; loyalty as a two-way compact.
- **Data, without being ruled by it.** Adopting sports science and analytics early, but keeping judgment and the human read in command.
- **Resilience and the long arc.** Building for the long term, absorbing setbacks, never losing sight of where the club was headed across decades.

## Moritz's epilogue — why Matt flagged it
~40 pages in which Moritz, drawing on 30 years as an operator/investor in Silicon Valley, maps Ferguson's approach to **building and sustaining a winning organization** onto how the best companies (and Sequoia itself) are built. The through-line: great leaders are far rarer than good managers, and their hallmark is a **relentless pursuit of their own idea of perfection**. He connects Ferguson's core practices — spotting and developing talent young, protecting standards, renewing the team imperceptibly, thinking in decades, never relinquishing control of the culture — to the venture craft of backing founders and building enduring institutions. It's essentially a bridge document between elite sports management and elite company-building.

## Why it's in Matt's canon
Reinforces themes that recur across his lenses and his people-reads: **spotting talent early and developing it** (Paradigm's "brilliant mutants," hiring young), **building an enduring institution over decades** (Moritz's "20-year increments"), **standards and the team-over-individual** discipline, and **judgment sitting above data**. Pairs naturally with the Moritz GSB talk and with Zero to One's founder/team material — the institution-building complement to Zero to One's 0-to-1 creation.
