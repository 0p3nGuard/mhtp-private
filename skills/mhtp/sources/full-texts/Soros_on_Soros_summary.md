# Soros on Soros: Staying Ahead of the Curve — George Soros, 1995
*Own-prose reference summary for MHTP (copyright-safe distillation, not verbatim). Source PDF received from Joe; held as reference. Part of Matt's canon.*

## Form
An extended interview-format book (with Byron Wien and Krisztina Koenen): three parts — Soros on investing, on politics/philanthropy (the open-society network), and on his philosophy. It's the accessible companion to his denser *The Alchemy of Finance*.

## Reflexivity (the central idea)
Soros rejects the efficient-market assumption that prices merely reflect fundamentals. His theory of **reflexivity** holds that there is a **two-way feedback loop** between participants' (biased) perceptions and the fundamentals they're perceiving: what people believe changes what they do, which changes the fundamentals, which changes what they believe. Markets are therefore often **far from equilibrium**, and perceptions can become self-validating for a while before they reverse. Prices don't just measure reality — they help *make* it.

## Boom / bust
From reflexivity comes his model of **self-reinforcing then self-defeating** processes — bubbles and crashes. A prevailing bias and an underlying trend reinforce each other until the gap between perception and reality becomes unsustainable, then the process runs in reverse. His edge was recognizing these distortions and their inflection points, not forecasting fundamentals in a straight line.

## Fallibility — thinking about his own thinking
The temperament that matters most for MHTP: Soros treats his own views as **hypotheses that are probably flawed**, and actively hunts for the error. He assumes he's wrong until proven otherwise, sizes up when the reflexive setup and risk/reward align, and — crucially — **cuts fast when the thesis breaks**. His self-description is that his success comes from recognizing his mistakes, not from being right more often. He even used physical discomfort (a backache) as a signal that something in the portfolio was off. Radical intellectual humility paired with aggressive conviction when the humility has been satisfied.

## Philosophy — the open society
Shaped by Karl Popper (Soros's LSE teacher): **critical rationalism** and the **open society** — no one has final truth, so societies (like minds) must stay self-correcting and open to falsification. This underwrites both his politics and his philanthropy (the Open Society foundations). Fallibility is the through-line from his markets to his worldview.
