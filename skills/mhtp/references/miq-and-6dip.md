# The MIQ Process, 6DIP, and MIQ Gap Analysis

Josh's own frameworks, in depth — the Most Important Question process, the Six Dimensional Introspective Process, and the gap analysis that pairs them. This file carries Josh's direct words wherever possible; quote him faithfully rather than paraphrasing.

---

## The MIQ Process

*"Thinking about this principle of scarcity, one of the ways that I have trained at this in the creative process is to have everyone whom I work with live in this routine. It's forcing yourself to, at the end of each day, think about what the most important question is in your work.*

*It's really interesting because when you're studying complexity all the time and, if you're a really high-level thinker, you're slicing most of this like butter. But then there's usually one, or two, or three layers of stuckness.*

*Most people, I find, tend to live in the creative process by surfacing, deciding where they want to go, putting their head down, and just grinding their way toward it, and then surfacing later on. They don't surface enough to reflect on what's the most potent direction to go.*

*If we're looking at everything, then we're just operating like really, really bad computers, but not if we cultivate the ability to ask the most potent questions systematically.*

*How do we do this? We have a routine where we end each workday thinking, 'What's the most important question in what I'm doing right now?' Pose the question to the unconscious mind, then leave it alone. Don't think about it. Then wake up first thing in the morning and brainstorm on it."*

*— Josh Waitzkin, Tim Ferriss Show, [Episode 148](https://youtu.be/jBu7gGTYzz0)*

### The daily mechanic

The MIQ process harnesses the power of writing to systematically and consciously invest in the quality of our thinking. It also constructs a bridge between the conscious and unconscious mind.

- **End of the working day:** set aside time for deliberate consideration. Stretch to your cognitive limits to identify the most important question of the day's work — the mental equivalent of an all-out sprint, a burst of maximum cognitive effort.
- **Release it:** Stress and Recovery in action. Deliberately avoid thinking about the question. Close the work day. While we sleep, the unconscious mind works over the question, producing insights we could not reveal had we not gotten out of its way.
- **First thing in the morning — pre-input** (before phones, email, reading, the needs of others): journal on the question, revealing whatever the unconscious unearthed in the night.

Consistent engagement documents the evolution of our thoughts, externalizes ideas to free up mental RAM, and provides a feedback loop through which we clarify our thinking.

### Stretching for the MIQ

*"Stretch for the MIQ as if it's driving your heart rate as high as it's ever been during the most intense workout of your life. That's how hard you're intellectually pushing for the MIQ. It's not just the question, it's really the fucking most important question. That's hard to get to, but stretch for that all the way.*

*And if you stretch for that all the way, you'll come upon something very interesting and then if you brainstorm on it next morning you'll have some ideas. And then if you return to it and you stretch for it the next day and then you observe the evolution of your MIQ.*

*And then you look at the gaps between them and you observe the patterns of the gaps and you call in a mentor to help you do your MIQ gap analysis personally, professionally, technically, psychologically, all the dimensions."*

*— Josh Waitzkin, [Fireside Chat 2019](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ycz1jRN0wvU)*

### Team MIQs

The MIQ Process serves as a cadence-setting touchpoint that syncs teams, catalyzing their creativity. Teams that embrace it see increased potency in their creativity, thinking, and questioning abilities — and begin to transform growth in singular domains into growth across all domains. Over time the process creates team resonance and cohesiveness as team members learn from and contribute to each other's thinking.

The shape teams have used successfully (Slack, Notion, Evernote, even Google Docs — the medium matters less than consistent engagement, consistent intensity of questioning, and the cultural courage and support to be wrong):

- Each team member poses an MIQ at the close of their workday.
- Pre-input the next morning, each riffs on their MIQ in the same channel for all to see.
- Periodically, team members review the MIQs of others, building shared consciousness.
- MIQs get tagged, categorized, and stored in a central repository.
- At intervals, the repository is reviewed by theme and category to assess the evolution of MIQs over time.

The Team MIQ process should not be performative. It's about using big questions to bring team members to the dynamic edge of their thinking and to their mental stretch point.

### What derails the MIQ process

- Emphasizing "right" answers rather than exploring questions at depth
- Fear of failure or looking foolish
- Lack of buy-in across a team
- Impeding the flow of another team member's idea (the objective is to amplify their thinking)
- Jumping in before a team member has concluded their train of thought
- Low-context comments — the default is not to interfere with someone's brainstorm flow unless you can add to their exploration

## The Six Dimensional Introspective Process (6DIP)

*"Once an artist reaches a certain level, and the foundation is internalized*
*Basic skills have been learned. Technique is solid and understood in the context of*
*Principles or themes that can help guide the mind through complexity*

*Commitment to the art is deep enough that we dream of what puzzles us, our stretch points, we find ourselves reflecting on our painting or music or chess position while walking down the street, driving, sitting, eating, watching, breathing…*

*At that moment that art and life dance together, and we are exploring our limits in the one to learn about ourselves in the other*

*Technical errors tend to arise when the psychology is stretched*

*Psychological errors arise when there is pressure on the mind from a lack of technical understanding*

*Each technique is housed in a principle that contains countless technical manifestations*

*Each theme or principle can be practiced through so many techniques*

*If there is a pattern of error in your professional or artistic life, it is most likely present in your personal relationships as well*

*If there is something haunting you in your personal life, it is most likely crimping your chess board, golf game, artistic or business career*

*If you look at the patterns of insight in your personal and professional (or academic) lives, and you break them down technically, thematically, and psychologically, what do you discover?*

*First list the insight*

*Do the same with errors*

*Make the 6 Dimensional Introspective Process a way of life, a breath pattern,*

*A cultivation of interconnectedness"*

*— Josh Waitzkin*

6DIP is a companion process to the MIQ Process that taps into the principles of Thematic Learning and Interconnectedness — which transform growth in one given dimension into growth in all dimensions.

The dimensions are three pairs:

- **Personal – Professional**
- **Technical – Psychological**
- **Tactical – Thematic**

**Personal – Professional:** Many separate their personal and professional lives, but they are actually connected. If we tend to avoid vulnerability with our partners and friends, we may do the same with colleagues. If we are averse to new foods or experiences, we may also be reluctant to invest in new processes or ideas at work. Dissolving the barrier between the two domains uncovers insights about our behavior that neither domain reveals alone.

**Technical – Psychological:** Study the relationship between the two — one can reveal important hidden weaknesses in the other. Technical errors often arise when the psychology is stretched: we tend to isolate the technical, but we usually make errors when our minds are overloaded, and it may not be the technical dimension that is maxing us out. Psychological errors often arise when there is pressure on the mind from a lack of technical understanding: when we haven't mastered the fundamentals in practice, we try to hold and process too much consciously, and we become overloaded.

Josh's example, from his early fight days in the Chinese martial arts:

*"I had the experience of a guy who would make me angry. He would make me angry by going after my nose, my jugular, my eyes — all outside the target area of the sport. I had this righteous indignation that came from me feeling like this guy's an asshole. He's not playing by the rules. All these self-protective things came out. The truth of the matter is I didn't know how to deal technically with what he was doing, therefore I was scared and I was having righteous indignation as an ego response.*

*So I took on training technically on how to deal with people attacking my eyes, my nose, my throat, my balls, and my knees, and just learned that technical part of the work. And then I just started inviting him to train all the time and I started asking the dirtiest guys I could find to come and fight with me. Then it was fine.*

*Technical weakness was leading to the psychological problem."*

*— [Fireside Chat 2019](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ycz1jRN0wvU)*

**Tactical – Thematic:** Each local, specific insight or technique is housed in a principle that contains countless specific manifestations. We can get stuck patching up the local issue — but if we take on the broader theme, train at it, turn it into a strength, then the countless local branches become natural to us. When reflecting on the patterns of your gaps, search for the principle that encompasses the tactical.

### Team 6DIP

*"Helpful feedback is accurate*
*Sometimes it is precise, sometimes abstract*
*It might dance between the Technical, Thematic, Psychological*
*A catalyst for Reframing?*
*Calling out self deception, self protection*

*Focused on your growth edge…*
*If it makes you a little uncomfortable, that is good"*

*— Josh Waitzkin*

Like the MIQ Process, 6DIP scales to teams. At predetermined intervals, during moments of curiosity, or both, team members evaluate team MIQs, searching for the gaps between them. Identifying the gaps — and working to close them with training and creative solutions — drives the entire organization forward creatively and innovatively. What derails Team 6DIP: emphasizing answers or solutions over inquiry, fear of judgment, protectiveness of ideas, lack of buy-in across a team.

## MIQ Gap Analysis

The operational move that pairs the two frameworks. We can use 6DIP to evaluate our MIQs across dimensions: perhaps a question was asked from a singular vector — framed as purely personal, or purely technical. What are its professional, technical, psychological, tactical, and thematic aspects? Identifying the gaps provides the opportunity to refine our questioning and thinking.

*"If you build your creative process around the focusing on MIQs, the channeling of that into the unconscious, riffing on it, you can drive your creative process that way.*

*And then if you also track the evolution of your MIQs around certain key questions, then you'll develop over time this log of gaps. And then you have to rhythmically look at what are the patterns around the gaps technically and psychologically. And then the patterns and the gaps become what you focus your deliberate practice on."*

*— Josh Waitzkin, [Fireside Chat 2019](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ycz1jRN0wvU)*

In practice, when the user asks for an MIQ gap analysis against shared material: pull the MIQs from the sources, lay them side by side in time, and surface the gaps through the three dimensional pairs. The patterns in the gaps are often where the real signal lives. Hold the gap as a hypothesis — the interesting work is what's *in* the gap: what changed, what didn't, what shifted underneath.

## Deeper Context: Josh on Training the Conscious–Unconscious Channel

### Connecting the conscious and unconscious mind

*"Ernest Hemingway wrote about how he always ended his workday leaving something left to write, ending his writing with the sense of direction, not writing everything. Most people have this external voice or this guilt voice operating inside them, telling them to do everything that they need to do. You're going to write everything you have to write and then stop. Hemingway would always end with a sense of direction and then release it. He would then wake up in the morning and his unconscious had worked on where he was and then he would just start flying.*

*And so for me, that turned into a habit structure around working, ending my workday, focusing my mind on what's the most important question, where's my mind potentially stuck? And not trying to answer that question, but just giving my unconscious something to work on. I wake up first thing in the morning and I journal. I return to that MIQ and start riffing on it.*

*And it's fascinating how if you do that and you develop just that as a way of life, you open up this channel systematically between your conscious and your unconscious mind. You learn to feed your unconscious questions to work on and then tap into what it's working on… You can do that four or five, 10 times a day, 20 times a day before you go to the bathroom, before you do a workout, before you take a cold shower, before you take a drink of water, anything, and pose a question."*

*— [Fireside Chat 2019](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ycz1jRN0wvU)*

### Breaking down the boundaries

*"I think that learning who you are as a learner is incredibly difficult. I think, obviously, it's great to have an ecosystem around you that can help you understand it. If you've got people who can really take you for who you are, as opposed to putting their own constructs on you, which is very difficult. Because people are trying to justify their own decisions. So they're trying to box you into those.*

*There's so many frames in order to understand who we are. Are we a visual kinesthetic or auditory processor? Most people don't even ask this question. Do you love mountains or ocean or city? Do you like the rain? Do you like to control things or let things rip in a more relaxed way? What are the patterns behind your greatest successes, and what are the patterns behind your biggest errors?*

*I like to look at that personally, professionally, technically, and psychologically. In other words, breaking down the boundaries between your personal life and your professional life, and looking at things both in terms of technical specific errors and brilliant creations and more thematic and psychological kind of meta manifestations of that tactical example."*

*— Tim Ferriss Show, [Episode 375](https://tim.blog/2019/07/03/the-tim-ferriss-show-transcripts-josh-waitzkin-how-to-cram-2-months-of-learning-into-1-day-375/)*

### The gold mine of intuition

*"My intuition is my most important guide. And I think that intuition can be a superpower if we cultivate it as a way of life and we systematically train it as a way of life. But if we don't, then it can be a train wreck. Because when it's not intuition that's driving us, it's just all of our ego protective instincts driving us.*

*I believe in training intuition as a way of life, which is hard. Our unconscious mind is so much more powerful than our conscious mind. And a lot of what I've described today in terms of opening systematically, building the channel between our conscious and unconscious mind, deconstructing our decision making processes, doing MIQ gap analysis to refine our intuition and refine our vision of what matters most.*

*Think about things personally, professionally, technically, and psychologically so that we are just relentlessly introspectively, deconstructing where our strengths and weaknesses are and where the patterns are. And then understanding when we have mental models, not just those mental models, but what are the exceptions of those rules and the exceptions of those rules and the exceptions of exception. If that's how you're living, I think intuition is an absolute gold mine."*

*— [Fireside Chat 2019](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ycz1jRN0wvU)*
